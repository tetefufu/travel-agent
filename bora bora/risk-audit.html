Risk & Stress Audit

Dubai → California → Bora Bora, 2027 · booked itinerary: St. Regis 15–20 May, flight option 3F, no buffer night

1. Flights

  • 🟢DXB↔LAX leg is solid. Qatar Airways, direct, business class, verified fare — reputable airline, no connection risk on the hardest leg.
  • 🟡LAX→SFO hop on Day 2 is low-stakes, and it's the smart routing choice. It lands on arrival day when nothing depends on it, instead of before the Bora Bora flight. Keep as-is.
  • 🔴Day 17 is the real problem. BOB→PPT→LAX→DXB, three legs, one calendar day. PPT→LAX is typically a late-night departure (~22:45–23:55), meaning most of the day is dead time in Papeete before an overnight crossing straight into a US entry process and the DXB long-haul.
  • 🔴The Tahiti legs are a separate ticket from the Qatar DXB↔LAX fare. No shared PNR means no rebooking protection — if the PPT→LAX flight runs late, Qatar owes you nothing.
  • 🟢flywithaccess.com verdict: low-risk, not clean (young domain, hidden owner) but not a scam. Moot — your selected 3F option uses the direct, verified Qatar fare, not flywithaccess quotes.

2. Fatigue & comfort

  • 🟢Outbound is fine — one direct business-class long-haul, then 10 relaxed California days before anything strenuous.
  • 🟡Three back-to-back one-night hotels (Carmel → Big Sur → Santa Barbara), stacked with a 4–5hr Highway 1 drive. Not dangerous, just the part most likely to feel like a chore.
  • 🔴Day 17 is the fatigue cliff. Dawn BOB→PPT hop, a full day waiting in Papeete, an overnight crossing, a full US immigration/customs/re-screening cycle with zero sleep, then straight onto a ~16hr flight to Dubai.

3. Passport, visa, immigration

  • 🟢UK passport → French Polynesia: visa-free up to 90 days. Keep passport valid 6+ months beyond travel, 2+ blank pages.
  • 🟢US entry: valid ESTA covers both touches on US soil (Day 1/2 arrival and the Day 17 connection) — a transit-only connection still legally counts as entering the US, same ESTA just needs to still be valid.
  • 🔴LAX has no sterile international transit. On Day 17 you personally clear Passport Control, collect checked bags, walk them through Customs, re-check, then go back through TSA — all before the DXB flight. Standard CBP policy, not an edge case.
  • 🟢French Polynesia entry/exit formalities are standard airport process at Papeete both directions.

4. Hotels

  • 🟡Day 12 Bora Bora arrival: if the LAX→PPT leg runs delayed, you risk missing St. Regis's scheduled resort-boat window. Confirm their late-arrival transfer policy before flying.
  • 🟢St. Regis + Westin split option (unselected in the data) would add a mid-stay transfer and unconfirmed cost — good that it's not in use, don't reconsider it for a 5-night stay.

5. Car rental

  • 🟡One-way SF→LA drop (pick up Carmel, return LA) almost always carries a drop-off surcharge — not currently budgeted.
  • 🟢UK licence is valid to drive in California without an IDP (it's in English) — bring it anyway in case a rental counter asks regardless.
  • 🟢No rental risk in French Polynesia — no driving there.

6. The one fix that matters most

The planner already has the answer built in, unticked: the buffer-night checkbox, whose own code comment says it exists to "de-risk the BOB→PPT→hub→DXB same-day connection." Tick it. One extra LAX hotel night turns Day 17's three-leg marathon into BOB→PPT→LAX (sleep) → LAX→DXB the next day — far cheaper than a missed, unprotected Dubai flight.

7. Other things worth knowing

  • 🟢Season is good — May is inside Bora Bora's dry season and outside the Nov–Apr Pacific cyclone season.
  • 🟡San Francisco timing is lucky but borderline — fog season ramps up through May, peaks June/July. Early May should still give decent Golden Gate views.
  • 🔴Buy missed-connection travel insurance specifically covering the separately-ticketed Tahiti leg.
  • 🟡Carry resort contact info and rental confirmation offline — Bora Bora/Papeete connectivity can be patchy.
